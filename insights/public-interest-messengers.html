From brand fit to public trust

How AI could help city agencies and nonprofits find the right trusted messenger for every community, message, and mission.

Share
X LinkedIn
JasmineAugust 2026Perspective

The opportunity

Public communication is a trust problem before it is a reach problem.

City agencies and nonprofits rarely lack important messages. They need residents to understand a new service, prepare for an emergency, attend an event, use a benefit, support a cause, or take part in civic life.

The harder question is who should carry each message. A large creator may generate attention but have little connection to the people affected. A smaller community voice may bring far more credibility, language fluency, local knowledge, and willingness to act.

That creates a natural extension for Jasmine. The product already evaluates how a creator's audience, content, values, performance, and risks fit a campaign brief. For public-interest work, the same decision can be reframed from brand-to-creator fit to mission-to-messenger fit.

The central question Who can carry this message credibly, safely, and accessibly to the community that needs it?

For city agencies

One city contains many different communication contexts.

A citywide campaign cannot assume that one creator, language, or channel will work equally well in every neighborhood. Jasmine could help an agency build a portfolio of trusted voices around the public objective.

01Public information

Match credible local communicators to public health, housing, transit, sanitation, workforce, education, or emergency-preparedness messages.

02Neighborhood relevance

Organize creator options by aggregated audience geography, language, community connection, content history, and evidence of local trust.

03Tourism and local economies

Find storytellers who can represent attractions, cultural institutions, neighborhoods, and small businesses with specificity rather than generic city imagery.

04Safety and accuracy

Surface misinformation, conflicting affiliations, harmful content, unsupported claims, and other risks before outreach begins.

05Operational accountability

Keep the brief, selection evidence, approvals, geographic coverage, deliverables, and outcomes visible across the agency and its vendors.

The result would not be a single list of “best NYC creators.” It would be a different, explainable coalition for each objective: heat safety in multiple languages, a small-business enrollment drive, a cultural tourism initiative, or a change in public services.

For nonprofits

Mission alignment can be more valuable than follower count.

Nonprofits often work with limited budgets and unusually high trust requirements. A creator who already understands the issue, serves the community, or has relevant lived experience may tell the story with more care and may be more motivated to participate.

01Mission storytellers

Turn the organization's purpose and impact into human stories without flattening the people or communities involved.

02Issue educators

Explain a complex topic through credible professional, community, or lived experience.

03Community connectors

Reach a particular place, language group, professional community, or interest network with genuine relevance.

04Action mobilizers

Support fundraising, volunteering, event attendance, service enrollment, or advocacy with a clear and appropriate call to action.

05Long-term ambassadors

Build sustained relationships with creators whose values and content remain aligned beyond a single post.

Jasmine could also make the reasoning easier to report to leadership, boards, funders, and partners: why each person was selected, which audience or role they served, what risks were reviewed, and what meaningful action followed.

Behind the scenes

AI organizes the evidence. People remain accountable for the decision.

The useful role for AI is not to declare who a community trusts. It is to help communications teams process a large, changing body of creator evidence consistently and make uncertainty visible.

1
Interpret the brief

Translate the objective, audience, geography, language, tone, accessibility needs, prohibited claims, and safety standards into campaign-specific criteria.

2
Build the candidate universe

Search beyond familiar contact lists to identify relevant local creators, practitioners, educators, and community voices.

3
Evaluate fit

Compare audience relevance, content themes, communication style, performance patterns, authenticity signals, and mission alignment.

4
Surface risk and uncertainty

Flag potential conflicts, unsafe content, unsupported claims, missing evidence, and questions that require human review.

5
Assemble a portfolio

Balance neighborhoods, languages, creator roles, channels, budget, and alternates rather than ranking everyone on one universal score.

6
Monitor and learn

Re-check creators as context changes, then connect campaign outcomes back to the decisions that produced them.

The guardrails

Public-interest use requires a higher standard of care.

Public agencies and nonprofits cannot treat creator selection as a black-box optimization exercise. The process should be explainable, contestable, accessible, and governed by people who understand the community and the legal environment.

Human controlAgency, nonprofit, community, and legal reviewers approve every activation.
PrivacyNo individual profiling or inference of sensitive traits.
ExplainabilityEvery recommendation includes evidence, uncertainty, and reasons for fit.
AccessibilityLanguage, format, disability access, and channel choice are part of the brief.
Community contextLocal partners validate what platform data cannot know.
Ongoing reviewRisk, eligibility, disclosures, and performance are rechecked throughout the campaign.

Jasmine should strengthen the judgment of communications teams and community partners, not replace it. The system's value is a clearer decision trail: what the brief required, what evidence was considered, where uncertainty remained, and who approved the final choice.

A practical pilot

Start with one real public-interest campaign.

A useful first pilot could focus on one objective, a small number of priority communities, and a clearly defined action. The organization would bring its communications team, community context, accessibility requirements, and approval rules. Jasmine would bring the creator evidence and decision framework.

ScopeOne campaign, three to five communities, and two or three languages.
DecisionAn explained shortlist with creator roles, alternates, and risk flags.
Process measuresTime to shortlist, geographic coverage, and first-round approval.
Outcome measuresService use, attendance, sign-ups, donations, volunteers, or verified information uptake.
