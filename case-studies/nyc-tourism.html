How Jasmine helped an immersive NYC attraction sell the feeling, not just the view

A campaign-specific creator cohort designed around visitor intent, differentiated storytelling, and the realities of operating a public attraction.

About this story: This is an illustrative pilot scenario based on common tourism-marketing workflows. It does not identify a Jasmine client or report actual campaign performance.
CampaignSummer evening experience
AudienceNYC locals and domestic visitors
ObjectiveDistinctive storytelling that drives visits
PlatformsInstagram and TikTok

The challenge

A famous skyline can make every shortlist look the same.

The attraction had no shortage of travel creators. The harder question was who could make this particular experience feel different from every other stop on a New York itinerary.

Follower count and broad travel affinity could identify people with reach, but they could not reveal who had the visual language to capture awe, who reached people able to visit, or who had recently promoted a competing attraction.

The brand manager also needed a shortlist that worked across two jobs: persuade New Yorkers to see their city differently and help visitors move the experience from “interesting” to “must book.”

The decision Jasmine reframed Not “Who posts about New York?” but “Who can turn this experience into a reason to go now?”

The brief

Jasmine turned brand context into campaign criteria.

The team entered the experience description, seasonal objective, target visitor, geographic priorities, creative guardrails, and venue-safety requirements.

Jasmine used that context to organize the creator decision around evidence that mattered for this campaign:

01Visitor relevance

Audience geography, language, age, and signals of near-term New York travel or local activity.

02Storytelling fit

Ability to build an emotional narrative around place, art, architecture, light, and shared experience.

03Content behavior

Recent view consistency, saves, completion, comment quality, and performance on comparable experiences.

04Safety and conflicts

Prior attraction partnerships, risky filming behavior, disclosure patterns, and fit with venue rules.

The cohort

One campaign needed three different kinds of influence.

Instead of producing one universal ranking, Jasmine organized the strongest candidates by the role each would play in the visitor journey.

01The visual storyteller

Creates an emotional, cinematic account of the experience that earns attention and establishes distinction.

02The local curator

Reaches New Yorkers with trusted recommendations for date nights, celebrations, and rediscovering the city.

03The itinerary planner

Turns inspiration into practical intent through timing, location, trip planning, and clear reasons to book.

Each recommendation included fit reasons, relevant evidence, audience differences, potential conflicts, and uncertainty. The brand manager and agency retained final approval.

The outcome

A shortlist designed for a decision, not a directory.

Before activation, the team had a focused creator portfolio with a clear purpose for every candidate. Creative differences were intentional, direct conflicts were visible, and the reasoning could travel from agency recommendation to brand approval.

A live pilot would measure the operational and campaign effects rather than treating reach as the only definition of success.

Decision efficiencyTime from brief to approved shortlist
Creator responseAcceptance and negotiation quality
Content qualityFirst-round approval and distinctiveness
Visitor intentSaves, qualified engagement, and ticket actions
What the brand manager learned The strongest tourism creator is not always the person with the largest travel audience. It is the person who can make this experience matter to the right visitor.
