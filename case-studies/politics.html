How Jasmine helped a statewide campaign build a trusted creator coalition

A campaign system designed around local credibility, geographic coverage, issue expertise, platform eligibility, and human legal approval.

About this story: This is a politically neutral, illustrative pilot scenario based on common U.S. campaign workflows. It does not identify a Jasmine client, endorse a candidate, or report actual campaign performance. Campaign counsel must approve all legal and compliance decisions.
CampaignOpen-seat statewide midterm race
CoverageUrban, suburban, and rural regions
ObjectiveAwareness, trusted discussion, and participation
ControlCampaign, agency, and counsel approval

The challenge

Political reach was abundant. Local trust was uneven.

The campaign and its agencies had assembled a large mix of political commentators, local personalities, issue experts, entertainers, and community voices.

A creator could support the candidate and still be wrong for the activation. Their audience might live outside the state, their style might be too combative for the assigned message, or their history might contain conflicting affiliations, unsupported claims, or platform restrictions.

The campaign manager needed to understand who could credibly carry each message, where their audience was concentrated, and what risk or disclosure obligation followed the recommendation.

The decision Jasmine reframed Not “Who agrees with the campaign?” but “Who can responsibly carry this message to the right part of the state?”

The brief

One statewide strategy became a set of local creator decisions.

The campaign gave Jasmine the candidate positioning, approved policy language, statewide objective, geographic priorities, relevant languages, creative tone, prohibited claims, conflict definitions, platform rules, and approval responsibilities.

Jasmine organized the evaluation around campaign-specific evidence while avoiding individual voter profiling and sensitive-trait inference.

01Geographic relevance

Aggregated in-state audience concentration, regional strength, language, and evidence of genuine local connection.

02Message credibility

Knowledge, lived or professional experience, communication history, and fit with the assigned campaign issue.

03Content capability

Ability to tell a personal story, explain policy, cover an event, or communicate accurate participation information.

04Safety and conflicts

Misinformation, hateful or violent rhetoric, harassment, undisclosed relationships, conflicting candidates, and unresolved uncertainty.

05Compliance readiness

Platform eligibility, disclosure placement, recordkeeping, pre-approval, and willingness to follow campaign and counsel requirements.

The coalition

Different forms of trust served different campaign jobs.

Jasmine produced a portfolio of creator roles instead of one statewide leaderboard. Each recommendation included fit evidence, geographic coverage, conflicts, uncertainty, and the channel on which activation was permitted.

01Statewide storytellers

Introduce the candidate's character and priorities through broadly accessible, human narratives.

02Local trusted voices

Connect statewide themes to particular cities, counties, industries, and community contexts.

03Issue practitioners

Explain topics through genuine professional or lived experience rather than generic political commentary.

04Civic educators

Communicate accurate registration, early-voting, absentee-ballot, and Election Day information from official sources.

05Mobilization partners

Reach audiences likely to attend events, volunteer, join campaign lists, or contribute, without sensitive-trait targeting.

The operating model

The shortlist changed as the campaign changed.

Each week, campaign leadership set the message and priority regions. Jasmine re-ranked eligible creators against that context, communications reviewed narrative fit, counsel reviewed claims and disclosures, and the agency activated only approved candidates.

Ongoing monitoring surfaced new conflicts, message drift, safety concerns, or changes in platform eligibility. Human reviewers could remove, pause, or replace a creator without rebuilding the full program.

At national midterm scale, the same model becomes three connected layers: national standards, a race-specific brief, and a local creator overlay. That structure exposes coverage gaps, creators appearing across conflicting shortlists, and race-specific compliance requirements.

The scaling principle One national standard. A different creator decision for every race, region, issue, and week.

Compliance

Channel eligibility came before creator activation.

Jasmine treated compliance as a visible decision layer, not a final checkbox. The campaign and its counsel remained responsible for interpreting federal and state law. The platform references below are current as of August 2026 and must be rechecked before activation.

FECCommittee disclaimers

Public communications by political committees generally require an appropriate payer disclaimer. Federal Election Commission guidance

GOOVerified election advertising

Google requires U.S. election-ad verification, restricts targeting, and requires payer and qualifying synthetic-content disclosures. Google political-content policy

TIKNo compensated political creators

TikTok prohibits paid political advertising, including creators compensated for branded political content. TikTok political-advertising policy

LAWState-specific review

State disclosure, privacy, election, and synthetic-media rules may add requirements. Counsel approval is required before activation.

The outcome

A coalition designed for accountability, not just scale.

The campaign received a geographically balanced creator portfolio with clear roles, alternates, evidence, safety flags, channel eligibility, and approval status. The decision could move from campaign strategy through agency execution and legal review without losing its reasoning.

A live pilot would measure operational quality and meaningful campaign actions rather than treating impressions as the only result.
