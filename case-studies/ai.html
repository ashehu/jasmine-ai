How Jasmine helped an AI company choose credibility over hype

A creator cohort designed around real practitioner experience, audience readiness, demonstrable workflows, and responsible product claims.

About this story: This is an illustrative pilot scenario based on common AI product-launch workflows. It does not identify a Jasmine client or report actual campaign performance.
CampaignAI workflow assistant launch
AudienceOperations and knowledge-work teams
ObjectiveTrust, understanding, and qualified trials
ActivationWorkflow demos, webinars, and short-form video

The challenge

Talking about AI was easy. Earning trust was harder.

The company found thousands of creators using AI terminology. Their expertise, audience relevance, and willingness to discuss limitations varied dramatically.

Some candidates could attract broad attention but had a history of overstating automation, accuracy, or business impact. Others were technically credible but spoke to developers rather than the operational buyers the product needed to reach.

The brand manager needed creators who could demonstrate the product honestly, explain where human judgment remained essential, and build confidence without flattening the story into another “AI changes everything” post.

The decision Jasmine reframed Not “Who is talking about AI?” but “Whose audience trusts them to show what this product can and cannot do?”

The brief

Technical depth was matched to audience readiness.

The team gave Jasmine the intended user, supported workflows, product limitations, approved claims, security considerations, competitive context, and the level of AI understanding expected from the audience.

Jasmine turned that context into a campaign-specific evaluation:

01Practitioner credibility

Evidence of real implementation, thoughtful evaluation, technical accuracy, and willingness to discuss tradeoffs.

02Audience readiness

Whether the creator reached developers, business leaders, practitioners, or general users, and what each group needed explained.

03Workflow proof

Ability to demonstrate a complete use case, include human review, and connect product behavior to a credible outcome.

04Claim discipline

Past exaggeration, disclosure behavior, competitor relationships, sensitive topics, and language requiring brand review.

The cohort

The campaign needed authority at more than one level.

Jasmine organized the strongest candidates by the kind of trust they could create and the audience decision they could support.

01The practitioner educator

Shows a real workflow, names the limits, and helps peers understand where the product belongs in daily work.

02The technical evaluator

Tests underlying capabilities and tradeoffs for audiences that require greater depth before believing the claim.

03The business translator

Connects the workflow to team-level value without making technical or financial promises the evidence cannot support.

Each recommendation showed the creator's appropriate level of claim, the audience they could credibly influence, and the questions a human reviewer still needed to resolve.

The outcome

A campaign designed to make trust part of performance.

The final cohort balanced technical authority, practical education, and business relevance. The team could tailor briefs by creator role, keep unsupported claims out of the campaign, and make brand-safety review visible before content production.

A live pilot would evaluate not only reach and trial volume, but whether the campaign produced informed interest from the people the product was built to serve.

What the brand manager learned In AI marketing, credibility is not a constraint on reach. It is what turns attention into informed adoption.
