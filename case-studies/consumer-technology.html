How Jasmine helped a technology brand find creators who could actually explain the product

A launch cohort built around product understanding, useful demonstrations, audience relevance, and the claims each creator could credibly make.

About this story: This is an illustrative pilot scenario based on common consumer-technology launch workflows. It does not identify a Jasmine client or report actual campaign performance.
CampaignNew productivity device launch
AudienceHybrid professionals and early adopters
ObjectiveProduct understanding and qualified demand
ActivationReviews, demonstrations, and short-form video

The challenge

Launch-day attention did not guarantee product understanding.

The brand could identify creators with large technology audiences. What it could not see clearly was who would move beyond specifications and show why the product mattered in a real working day.

Some candidates specialized in rapid unboxings. Others had strong production quality but little experience testing products over time. Several reached broad gadget audiences rather than the professionals most likely to adopt the device.

The brand manager needed creators capable of earning attention, demonstrating the workflow accurately, and staying inside product and performance claims.

The decision Jasmine reframed Not “Who covers technology?” but “Who can make the product useful, understandable, and credible to the intended user?”

The brief

Product workflows became creator-selection evidence.

The team gave Jasmine the launch objective, primary user, core workflows, approved product claims, competitive context, creative requirements, and the distinct jobs paid and earned creators needed to perform.

Jasmine organized the decision around four campaign-specific dimensions:

01Product fluency

Depth of prior explanations, accuracy, testing behavior, and ability to translate features into practical value.

02User relevance

Audience profession, interests, device behavior, workflow needs, geography, and signals of category intent.

03Demonstration quality

Clarity of hands-on content, real-use scenarios, retention, saves, comment quality, and useful follow-up.

04Claims and conflicts

Competitor relationships, unsupported performance language, disclosure behavior, and unresolved safety concerns.

The cohort

Different creators carried different parts of the product story.

Jasmine separated launch reach from product education and gave each recommended creator a defined role in the campaign.

01The hands-on reviewer

Tests the product seriously, explains tradeoffs, and gives an informed audience confidence in the recommendation.

02The workflow educator

Shows the device solving a recognizable problem inside a practical, repeatable routine.

03The lifestyle translator

Makes the product relevant beyond technology enthusiasts without reducing the story to appearance or novelty.

The final comparison showed what each creator could explain, who they could credibly influence, and where claims or competitive history required human review.

The outcome

A launch plan designed to create understanding, not just impressions.

The brand manager received an explainable portfolio of reviewers, educators, and translators rather than a single ranking dominated by reach. The agency could brief each creator against a specific communication job and resolve risk before contracting.

A live pilot would connect shortlist quality to both operational efficiency and evidence that audiences understood the product.

Decision efficiencyTime from brief to approved launch cohort
Creator readinessAcceptance and quality of briefing questions
Product clarityCompletion, saves, and informed audience comments
Demand qualityQualified clicks, product-page behavior, and intent
What the brand manager learned The best product creator is not necessarily the person who reaches the most technology fans. It is the person who can make the right user understand why the product belongs in their life.
